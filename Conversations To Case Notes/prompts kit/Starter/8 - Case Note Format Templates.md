# Case Note Format Templates

**What this does.** Five case note formats. Pick one, name it in the FORMAT line of *6 - Draft Case Note Prompt*, and you get a note in that shape.

**How to choose.** Use the format your program or funder expects. **General-purpose is the safe default** for non-clinical social services. SOAP, DAP, and GIRP come from programs with a clinical or behavioural-health lineage. Case Amplify mirrors the structure used in that system.

**What to check.** That the format actually matches your program's documentation standard, and that any "Assessment" section stays non-clinical — needs and eligibility reasoning, never diagnosis.

---

## ⚠️ Before you use SOAP, DAP, or GIRP

In clinical settings, an **"Assessment"** section can imply a diagnosis, and many programs restrict who is permitted to write one at all.

The adaptations below deliberately treat Assessment as *needs and eligibility reasoning* and label it non-clinical. That works for benefits and employment case management. It may not satisfy a program with clinical documentation rules, and **clinical assessment requires a licensed writer.**

If you're not certain which standard applies to your program, use General-purpose and ask your supervisor.

---

All five examples below document the *same* fictional conversation, so you can compare them side by side. All names, dates, and numbers are invented.

---

## 1. General-purpose (non-clinical) — *default*

**Sections:** Contact & Context · Presenting Needs · Actions Taken · Plan & Next Steps

**Use when:** general social services, benefits, or employment case management, with no clinical framing needed.

*The fully worked example is in* **6 - Draft Case Note Prompt**.

## 2. SOAP

**Sections:** Subjective · Objective · Assessment · Plan

*Non-clinical adaptation: "Objective" means verified or observable case facts, not a physical exam. "Assessment" means needs and eligibility reasoning, not a diagnosis.*

> **S (Subjective — client's report).** Client reports a layoff from warehouse work (last day July 18) due to downsizing; states prior pay ~$18.50/hr at ~32 hrs/week. Reports a repetitive-strain wrist injury she attributes to the job and says it limits physical work. Expresses concern about rent ($1,150/mo) and reports a landlord late notice. States interest in medical-billing or administrative work; reports a high-school diploma and ~1 year of community college. Requests childcare for her 4-year-old to enable a return to work.
>
> **O (Objective — verified/observable).** Identity and household verified. Client ID CL-2026-04417. Household: client plus two dependents (ages 9 and 4). Current SNAP benefit $535/mo. SNAP recertification due Aug 22. Workforce orientation scheduled Aug 14, 10:00 AM.
>
> **A (Assessment — non-clinical / eligibility).** Income reduction is expected to affect the SNAP benefit at recertification. Client appears potentially eligible for the childcare subsidy and one-time rental assistance given the job loss. Stated background and interest align with the workforce program's administrative and medical-office tracks. *(Programmatic assessment only; no clinical diagnosis.)*
>
> **P (Plan).** (1) Caseworker emails rental-assistance form today. (2) Client returns recertification paperwork by Aug 22. (3) Childcare-subsidy application submitted by Aug 20. (4) Client attends orientation Aug 14, 10:00 AM.
>
> ---
>
> ##### Review & sign-off
>
> **Stated vs. inferred:** Dates resolve to 2026 (session dated Aug 5, 2026). "Objective" items are verified case facts, not clinical measurements. "Assessment" is eligibility reasoning, not a determination.
>
> **Open questions / missing info:** Confirm recertification documentation; confirm subsidy deadline.

## 3. DAP

**Sections:** Data · Assessment · Plan — *Data combines what SOAP splits into Subjective and Objective.*

Client reports a layoff from warehouse work (last day July 18; prior ~$18.50/hr, ~32 hrs/wk) and a repetitive-strain wrist injury limiting physical work. ## 4. GIRP

**Sections:** Goal · Intervention · Response · Plan

> **G (Goal).** Client's stated goals: keep SNAP benefits current through recertification, secure childcare to enable a return to work, transition into non-physical work (medical billing or administrative), and stabilize housing.
>
> **I (Intervention).** Caseworker verified household and income, explained the recertification income-change process, referred the client to a workforce-development program and scheduled orientation (Aug 14, 10:00 AM), initiated a childcare-subsidy application, and identified one-time rental-assistance eligibility.
>
> **R (Response).** Client engaged and agreeable; confirmed she will attend the Aug 14 orientation and return the recertification paperwork; expressed intent to proceed with the childcare and rental-assistance applications.
>
> **P (Plan).** Email rental-assistance form today; client returns recertification by Aug 22; submit childcare-subsidy application by Aug 20; attend orientation Aug 14.
>
> ---
>
> ##### Review & sign-off
>
> **Stated vs. inferred:** Dates resolve to 2026 (session dated Aug 5, 2026). "Response" reflects the client's stated agreement in the read-back, not an interpretation of affect.
>
> **Open questions / missing info:** Confirm the client has transportation to the Aug 14 orientation.
>
> **Reviewed by:** ──────────────────────   **Date:** ──────────────
>
> *AI-drafted. Not part of the record until reviewed and signed by a caseworker.*

> **Watch the Response section.** "Client engaged and agreeable" is close to the line — it records what she said, but it's one small step from characterizing her mood, which the Master Instructions forbid. If a draft comes back with "client seemed motivated" or "client was upbeat," edit it. Record what a person said or agreed to, not how they struck you.

## Comparing the five

Read them next to each other and you'll notice the facts never change — only the shape does. That's the useful insight: the format is a container your program chose, not a different account of what happened.

Which means picking a format is a compliance and program question, not a writing question. Ask what your funder or program requires, use that, and don't switch formats mid-caseload because one reads better.

**Adding your own format.** If your program uses something not listed here, you don't need a new prompt. Write out your section headings, paste them under the FORMAT line in *6 - Draft Case Note Prompt*, and tell the AI to follow those sections exactly. The guardrails in the Master Instructions still apply.
